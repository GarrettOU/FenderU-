# How lake boats actually hang fenders (art direction for FenderU images)

Checked 25 Oct 2026 against the pages and photos below. Each rule is tagged **[confirmed: source]** when a page or photo we opened says it, or **[unconfirmed]** when no source we could reach says it. Source short names match the "Reference pages" list.

The reference photos are for art direction only. They show what correct rigging looks like. Never composite FenderU covers onto them (standing rule: use only Garrett's own photos).

## Every boat type

- **Orientation:** vertical against a flat dock, a floating dock, a seawall or another boat. Horizontal against a single piling or post. [confirmed: BoatUS, Boating Mag hang, Discover Boating fenders]
- **Height at a floating dock:** the bottom of the fender sits just above the waterline, a few inches up, so it stays out of the water. [confirmed: Boating Mag hang, Discover Boating fenders, Camping World]
- **Height in general:** set the fender where the hull meets whatever you're lying against, and adjust it after you tie up. [confirmed: BoatUS, Discover Boating fenders]
- **Where along the boat:** at least one fender at the widest part of the hull, with more forward and aft. [confirmed: BoatUS, Discover Boating fenders, Camping World]
- **Tie point:** a cleat is best. A rail also works, but tie at the bottom of the stanchion, where the rail is strongest and has the least leverage. A clove hitch goes on a rail, a cleat hitch on a cleat. [confirmed: BoatUS, Boating Mag hang, Discover Boating fenders, Overton's]
- **Never tie to the wake tower, the windshield or a bimini tube.** [unconfirmed. No page says this. It follows from the "low tie point, cleats first" advice.]
- **How many:** 2–4 per side on most boats, and never fewer than two. Taylor Made says one per 10 ft of waterline per side, with three minimum. [confirmed: Discover Boating fenders, Boating Mag hang, Taylor Made guide, Camping World]
- **Which side:** on the side going against the dock. In a slip, on both sides. [confirmed: Overton's]
- **Underway:** fenders come aboard. A boat running with its fenders out looks like a rookie's. [confirmed: Discover Boating fenders, Boating Mag tips, Pontoonopedia attach] (Pontoonopedia notes that many pontoon owners leave them hanging anyway, so for a pontoon this is common, but it still reads as wrong.)

## Pontoon / tritoon

- **Size:** 8.5" x 26" is **not** the standard pontoon size. Taylor Made's chart rates 8-1/2" x 26" for **25–35 ft** boats and 6-1/2" x 22" for 20–25 ft. So 8.5 x 26 is right on a 25 ft+ pontoon or tritoon, and an upsize on a 20–24 ft one. [confirmed: Taylor Made Super Gard chart] Sources say to go bigger when in doubt. [confirmed: Camping World, Discover Boating fenders]
- **How many:** two per side on a 20 ft pontoon, so four in all. [confirmed: Pontoonopedia fenders]
- **Where:** near the front gate and the rear gate. [unconfirmed] A fender at the bow corner [unconfirmed]. Taylor Made sells separate corner guards for that spot.
- **Tie point:** a line from the fence top rail, usually through a clamp-on rail adjuster or clip (Taylor Made's adjuster fits 1" and 1-1/4" square pontoon rails), or from an eye or bracket on a rail upright. [confirmed: Taylor Made adjuster, Pontoonopedia attach, photos 1–5]
- **Height:** the fender hangs outside the fence. Its top is near the deck edge, and its body covers the deck edge and the side of the tube, between the boat and the dock. [confirmed: photos 1–4; Taylor Made pontoon fenders "fit snugly between the pontoon and deck"]
- **Hanging too high, against the fence panel only, is the most common pontoon mistake.** [unconfirmed]

## Wake / ski boat and bowrider

- **Where:** fenders at the bow, midships and stern. [confirmed: Malibu]
- **Hang:** vertical, against the hull side, tied off at a cleat or the bow rail. [confirmed: photos 7–9]
- **Pop-up cleats are the tie point.** [unconfirmed for wake boats specifically; cleats in general are confirmed above]
- **Size:** about 6"–6.5" diameter for 20–25 ft boats. [confirmed: Boating Mag tips, Taylor Made Super Gard chart] WakeMAKERS' pick for wake boats is a 6" x 15" fender. [confirmed: WakeMAKERS] 8.5 x 26 fits 25 ft and longer [confirmed: Taylor Made chart]; on a 23–24 ft high-sided wake boat it is an upsize. [unconfirmed]

## Raft-up at a party cove or sandbar

- **Layout:** boats tie up side by side, all facing the same way, with swim platforms lined up so people can walk platform to platform. The largest boat anchors in the middle and the others alternate sides. [confirmed: Discover Boating raft-up, Grady-White, photos 11–15]
- **Fenders:** at the beamiest part of the boat (usually midships), where the two hulls touch. Use plenty, and big ones. [confirmed: Grady-White, Boating Mag hang]
- **How many:** at least one per 10 ft of boat length. [confirmed: Discover Boating raft-up]
- **Vertical between the boats.** [confirmed: BoatUS (vertical alongside another boat)]
- **Different-height boats:** one boat hangs its fenders lower and the other higher. [confirmed: Discover Boating fenders]
- **The boat already in the raft puts its fenders out for the next boat to arrive.** So a fender on an empty outer side is normal in a raft-up. [confirmed: Grady-White]
- **Lines:** tight, to cleats, never to handrails. [confirmed: Discover Boating raft-up, Grady-White]

## Mistakes that make an image look fake

- Fenders out while the boat is running. [confirmed: Discover Boating fenders, Boating Mag tips]
- A fender dragging in the water. [confirmed: Boating Mag hang (keep it a few inches above the surface)]
- A fender sitting above the contact zone. [confirmed: BoatUS, Discover Boating fenders]
- A horizontal fender against a flat dock. [confirmed: BoatUS, Boating Mag hang, Discover Boating fenders] (Boating Mag's buying-tips page has one sentence that says the opposite. Its sister article and every other source agree on vertical, so treat that sentence as an error.)
- A fender tied high on a rail top or tower instead of a cleat or the bottom of a stanchion. [confirmed for rails: BoatUS, Discover Boating fenders; unconfirmed for towers]
- A fender on the open side away from the dock, at a dock. [confirmed: Overton's] (Not a mistake in a raft-up; see above.)
- One lonely fender. [confirmed: Boating Mag hang (two minimum), Camping World (three minimum)]
- A long, slack fender line. [unconfirmed]

## What the first version got wrong

1. **"8.5 x 26 is the pontoon size."** Wrong. Taylor Made rates 8.5 x 26 for 25–35 ft boats. Most 20–24 ft pontoons fall in the 6.5" band. Pontoonopedia (a pontoon site) even prefers pontoon-specific fenders over cylinders.
2. **"One fender per 10 ft of boat plus one."** Nothing we read says "plus one." The sources say one per 10 ft (Discover Boating raft-up, Taylor Made per side), or 2–4 per side.
3. **"Boats line up swim platform to swim platform."** That suggested stern-to-stern. The raft-up sources mean side by side, with the swim platforms lined up.
4. **"At a floating dock, the lower third of the fender does the work"** and **"hung too high is the most common mistake."** No source says either one. They are dropped or marked unconfirmed.
5. **"A fender on the side away from the dock is a mistake."** True at a dock, but in a raft-up the boat already tied in puts out fenders for the next arrival.
6. **The tie-point rule.** The real rule is cleat first, then the bottom of a stanchion, not the rail top, because of leverage. "Never the tower or windshield" is a reasonable inference, but no source states it.
7. **The opening line said page fetches were blocked.** Every page below was opened and read on 25 Oct 2026.
