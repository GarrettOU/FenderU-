# How lake boats actually hang fenders (art direction for FenderU images)

Compiled 25 Oct 2026 from BoatUS, Boating Mag, Discover Boating, West Marine, Malibu, Grady-White, and Bennington / MasterCraft / pontoon owner forums. These come from search summaries: page fetches were blocked in this environment.

**Every boat type**
- **Orientation:** vertical against a dock, a floating dock or another boat. Horizontal only against a single piling.
- **Height:** the bottom of the fender sits a few inches above the waterline, and the fender is centered on the point where the boat touches the dock. At a floating dock, the lower third of the fender does the work.
- **Line:** a short line from the top eye to a cleat, a rail base or a stanchion. Never to the wake tower, the windshield or a thin bimini tube.
- **How many:** one fender per 10 ft of boat plus one. That's 3–4 per side on a lake boat: forward, at the widest point and aft.
- **Underway:** fenders come aboard. Fenders hanging out while running look wrong.

**Pontoon / tritoon. This is the best showcase, and 8.5 x 26 is the pontoon size.**
- 2–3 per side: near the front gate, near the rear gate, sometimes one at the bow corner.
- Clipped or tied to the fence top rail or a stanchion base on a short line.
- They hang outside the fence and down past the deck edge, so the fender covers the deck edge and the top of the tube, just above the water. Hung too high, a fender misses the deck edge. That's the most common mistake.

**Wake / ski boat (Malibu, MasterCraft, Nautique, Tigé)**
- Bow, midship and stern pop-up cleats. Vertical, hanging against the hull side.
- 6.5" is typical on 20–24 ft boats. Larger 23–25 ft hulls with high sides use 8.5 x 26.

**Raft-up at a party cove or sandbar**
- Fenders go between the boats at the widest point, vertical, 2–3 per side.
- Boats line up swim platform to swim platform.

**Mistakes that make an image look fake**
- Fenders out while the boat is underway.
- A fender dragging in the water.
- A fender sitting above the contact zone.
- A horizontal fender against a flat dock.
- A line tied to the tower or windshield.
- Long slack line.
- A fender on the side away from the dock.
- A single lonely fender.
